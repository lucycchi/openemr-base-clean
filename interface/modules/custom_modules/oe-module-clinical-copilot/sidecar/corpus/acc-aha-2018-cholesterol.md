# 2018 AHA/ACC cholesterol guideline (summary)

## Who benefits from statin therapy
Four groups have the clearest benefit from statin therapy: adults with clinical atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD); adults with LDL cholesterol of 190 mg/dL or higher; adults aged 40 to 75 with diabetes; and adults aged 40 to 75 without diabetes whose 10-year ASCVD risk is 7.5 percent or higher after a clinician-patient risk discussion. For an LDL of 190 mg/dL or higher, high-intensity statin therapy is recommended without a risk calculation.

## Intensity and LDL targets
High-intensity statin therapy (for example atorvastatin 40 to 80 mg or rosuvastatin 20 to 40 mg) aims to lower LDL by 50 percent or more. Moderate-intensity therapy lowers LDL by 30 to 49 percent. In very high-risk ASCVD, an LDL threshold of 70 mg/dL is used to consider adding ezetimibe and then a PCSK9 inhibitor. In primary prevention with diabetes, moderate-intensity statin is the starting point, escalated to high intensity when several risk factors are present.

## Risk assessment in primary prevention
For adults 40 to 75 without diabetes and LDL 70 to 189 mg/dL, estimate 10-year ASCVD risk with the pooled cohort equations. Risk below 5 percent is low; 5 to under 7.5 percent is borderline; 7.5 to under 20 percent is intermediate; 20 percent or higher is high. Risk-enhancing factors (family history of premature ASCVD, persistently elevated LDL of 160 mg/dL or higher, metabolic syndrome, chronic kidney disease, chronic inflammatory conditions, persistently elevated triglycerides of 175 mg/dL or higher) favor starting a statin in the borderline and intermediate groups. Coronary artery calcium scoring can help when the decision is uncertain.

## Monitoring
Repeat a lipid panel 4 to 12 weeks after starting or adjusting a statin to confirm the expected LDL response and adherence, then every 3 to 12 months. Baseline ALT is reasonable before starting; routine monitoring of liver enzymes is not required unless symptoms appear.

## Hypertriglyceridemia
Fasting or non-fasting triglycerides of 175 mg/dL or higher warrant addressing lifestyle factors, secondary causes and medications; persistent moderate elevation (175 to 499 mg/dL) in adults 40 to 75 with ASCVD risk of 7.5 percent or higher favors starting or intensifying a statin. Severe hypertriglyceridemia of 500 mg/dL or higher raises pancreatitis risk and calls for evaluation of secondary causes and, when 1000 mg/dL or higher, a very low fat diet and fibrate therapy.
