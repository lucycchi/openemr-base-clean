# ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2025 (summary)

## Diagnosis and screening
Diabetes is diagnosed by A1C of 6.5 percent or higher, fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher, a 2-hour plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test, or a random glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher with symptoms; an abnormal result is confirmed with a second test unless there are unequivocal symptoms. Prediabetes is A1C 5.7 to 6.4 percent, fasting glucose 100 to 125 mg/dL, or 2-hour glucose 140 to 199 mg/dL. Screen all adults from age 35, and earlier for adults with overweight or obesity and one or more risk factors; repeat at least every 3 years if normal.

## Glycemic targets
An A1C goal below 7 percent is appropriate for many nonpregnant adults without significant hypoglycemia. Less stringent goals, such as below 8 percent, are appropriate for people with limited life expectancy, advanced complications or extensive comorbidities. Measure A1C at least twice a year when at goal and stable, and quarterly when therapy has changed or goals are not met.

## Pharmacologic treatment of type 2 diabetes
Metformin remains a first-line agent alongside lifestyle. Independent of A1C, a GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor with demonstrated benefit is recommended for people with established or high risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease. Check kidney function before and periodically during metformin use; metformin is not started when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73m2 and dose is reassessed between 30 and 45.

## Chronic kidney disease in diabetes
Assess urinary albumin (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio) and eGFR at least annually in everyone with type 2 diabetes and in type 1 diabetes of 5 or more years. An SGLT2 inhibitor is recommended for people with type 2 diabetes and CKD with eGFR of 20 or higher to reduce CKD progression and cardiovascular events. An ACE inhibitor or ARB is recommended for nonpregnant people with diabetes and hypertension with albuminuria.

## Cardiovascular risk management
Treat blood pressure to a target below 130/80 mm Hg when it can be safely reached. Moderate-intensity statin therapy is recommended for people with diabetes aged 40 to 75 without ASCVD; high-intensity statin therapy for those at higher risk or with ASCVD; consider a statin for people 20 to 39 with additional risk factors.
