# 2017 ACC/AHA high blood pressure guideline (summary)

## Classification
Normal blood pressure is below 120/80 mm Hg. Elevated is systolic 120 to 129 with diastolic below 80. Stage 1 hypertension is systolic 130 to 139 or diastolic 80 to 89. Stage 2 hypertension is systolic 140 or higher or diastolic 90 or higher. Diagnosis rests on an average of two or more readings on two or more occasions, and out-of-office measurement (home or ambulatory) is recommended to confirm the diagnosis and titrate therapy.

## When to start medication
For stage 1 hypertension, start antihypertensive medication when the patient has clinical cardiovascular disease or an estimated 10-year ASCVD risk of 10 percent or higher; otherwise begin with lifestyle changes and reassess in 3 to 6 months. For stage 2 hypertension, start medication together with lifestyle changes, often with two agents of different classes.

## Treatment target
A blood pressure target of below 130/80 mm Hg is recommended for adults with known cardiovascular disease or a 10-year ASCVD risk of 10 percent or higher, and is reasonable for others with hypertension.

## First-line agents
First-line agents are thiazide diuretics, calcium channel blockers, ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers. In adults with chronic kidney disease stage 3 or higher, or stage 1 or 2 with albuminuria, an ACE inhibitor (or ARB) is reasonable to slow kidney disease progression. ACE inhibitors and ARBs must not be combined.

## Follow-up and monitoring
After starting or changing therapy, reassess monthly until control is reached, then every 3 to 6 months. Check electrolytes and kidney function 2 to 4 weeks after starting an ACE inhibitor, ARB or diuretic. Lifestyle measures with proven effect: weight loss, the DASH diet, sodium reduction below 1500 mg per day, potassium-rich foods, physical activity and limiting alcohol.
